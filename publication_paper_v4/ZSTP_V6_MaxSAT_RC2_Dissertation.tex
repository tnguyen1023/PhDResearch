\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

This paper presents \textbf{ZSTP-V6}, a Zone-Slot-Time-Persona honeypot placement framework
that formulates optimal deception-asset scheduling as a weighted partial MaxSAT problem
solved by the RC2 core-guided algorithm~\cite{imms2019rc2}. The framework operates across five
enterprise network zones (DMZ, Internal, Cloud, OT, Management), four attacker personas,
four planning time slots, and four adversarial threat scenarios
$\Theta{=}\{\theta_{\mathrm{low}}, \theta_{\mathrm{med}}, \theta_{\mathrm{high}}, \theta_{\mathrm{burst}}\}$,
producing a certified worst-case detection guarantee $\rstar$ together with a full set of
15 operational metrics.

Against ten competing baselines, RC2 achieves $\rstar{=}3{,}956$ (+26\% over the best
heuristic), covers all 18 ATT\&CK techniques across 8 tactic families, maintains zero
discovery burn ($\mathrm{PBurn}{=}0\%$, $\mathrm{TBurn}{=}0\%$), and wins 14 of 15 metrics
against every baseline — a grand total of \textbf{139/150 (93\%) metric$\times$baseline wins}.

The Section~G force-multiplier integration demonstrates that a single Internal-zone
deployment simultaneously earns L3 detection credit across three attack paths, achieving
$\FMeff{=}1.53\times$ versus greedy baselines that earn $\FMeff{\approx}0.45\times$ due to
discovery burn. The framework is validated at three levels: analytical optimisation
(XLarge), network process emulation (XXLarge: 39 live TCP detections, 8 STIX bundles across
8 slots), and theoretical proof of the $\rstar$ lower bound.

\begin{keyresult}
RC2 is the only solver in this comparison that simultaneously provides a certified
worst-case guarantee $\rstar$, zero discovery burn, full ATT\&CK technique breadth,
multi-path force-multiplier credit, and dynamic threat-intelligence adaptation. No
baseline can make all five claims simultaneously.
\end{keyresult}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}

Enterprise networks face persistent, adaptive adversaries who traverse multiple security
zones before reaching high-value assets. Traditional honeypot placement is static,
rule-based, and zone-agnostic: an operator places a decoy service in one segment and
leaves it indefinitely. This strategy has two critical flaws:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, label=(\arabic*)]
  \item A smart attacker learns to recognise static decoys after repeated visits,
        rendering them invisible — the \emph{discovery-burn problem}.
  \item It ignores the multi-path nature of modern campaigns, where the same zone is
        traversed by attackers following completely different routes.
\end{enumerate}

ZSTP-V6 addresses both flaws. It formulates honeypot placement as a combinatorial
optimisation problem over the full space of $(\text{trap-type},\text{zone},\text{slot},\text{persona})$
assignments, solved by the RC2 MaxSAT algorithm which produces a certified optimal
solution alongside a worst-case guarantee $\rstar = \min_{k \in \Theta} \Qkx$.

\begin{insight}
\textbf{Real-world analogy.} Think of five museum rooms (DMZ, Internal, Cloud, OT, Mgmt)
and three thieves who each take a different route but all end up in the vault (Internal).
One fake safe placed in the vault catches all three simultaneously — earning 1.63$\times$
more credit than a greedy solver that only considered the most likely thief. This is the
\emph{force-multiplier} (Section~G).
\end{insight}

\begin{equation}

\mathcal{I} = \bigl(K,\,T,\,A,\,Z,\,P,\,G,\,C,\,I_2,\,\diamond,\,w,\,\mathrm{cost},\,B,\,B_2,\,d_m,\,h^d,\,\sigma,\,\rho,\,iv,\;

               \gamma,\,\beta_{\max},\,\kappa,\,\tau_{GK},\,h_{\min},\,\kappa_{\min},\,\rho_{\mathrm{decay}},\,\Delta_N\bigr)
\end{equation}

Line~1 reproduces the original base-paper instance tuple unchanged.
Line~2 adds nine V1--V3 persona-layer parameters.
Line~3 adds eight V4--V5 gap-resolution parameters.
Equation~\eqref{eq:instance} is the single formal source of truth — every parameter
appearing anywhere in this document appears here.

\subsection{Decision Variable}

The sole decision variable is a binary assignment:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decvar}
\xiztp \;=\; 1 \quad\iff\quad \text{trap type } i\in K \text{ is deployed in zone } z\in Z
\text{ at slot } t \text{ targeting persona } p\in P.
\end{equation}
The variable space has size $|K|{\cdot}|Z|{\cdot}H{\cdot}|P| = 8{\cdot}5{\cdot}4{\cdot}4 = 640$
(XLarge) or $12{\cdot}7{\cdot}8{\cdot}6 = 4{,}032$ (XXLarge).

\subsection{Derived Binary Variables}
\label{sec:derived}

Five derived variables are computed by \texttt{DecisionVariables.compute\_all\_derived()}:

\begin{align}
p_{\pi,h,t} &= 1 \;\iff\; \exists(i,p):\; \xiztp{=}1 \;\wedge\; GK(i,p)\ge\tau_{GK} \;\wedge\; \dg{=}1
  \label{eq:ppath} \tag{3} \\
e_{\pi,t} &= 1 \;\iff\; \exists h < |\pi|{-}1:\; p_{\pi,h,t}{=}1
  \label{eq:eintercept} \tag{4} \\
u_{i,z,t} &= 1 \;\iff\; \sum_{s=t-\lceil\taud\rceil+1}^{t}\sum_p \xiztp \;\ge\; \lceil\taud\rceil
  \label{eq:utype} \tag{5} \\
u_{i,z,t,p} &= 1 \;\iff\; \sum_{s=t-\lceil\taudp\rceil+1}^{t} x_{i,z,s,p} \;\ge\; \lceil\taudp\rceil
  \label{eq:upersona} \tag{6} \\
\dg_{i,z,t,p} &= (1 - u_{i,z,t})\cdot(1 - u_{i,z,t,p})
  \label{eq:dualguard} \tag{7}
\end{align}

Variables~\eqref{eq:ppath}--\eqref{eq:dualguard} connect directly to dissertation metrics:
$u_{i,z,t}$ determines TBurn\%; $u_{i,z,t,p}$ determines PBurn\%;
$e_{\pi,t}$ determines Early\%; $p_{\pi,h,t}$ determines Hop\% and C10\%;
$\dg$ determines FM$_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Table~\ref{tab:metrics}).

\subsection{Per-Asset Detection Weight}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weight}
\Wja \;=\; w_j \;\cdot\; \frac{d_m(a)}{h^d(a)} \;\cdot\; (1 + \sigma_j)
\tag{8}
\end{equation}
where $h^d(a)\in\{1,2,3\}$ encodes zone depth: DMZ=1, Internal/Cloud=2, OT=3.
Deeper zones reduce $W$ — harder-to-reach assets are harder to instrument.
Computed by \texttt{DerivedWeights.zone\_avg\_W}$(z,j)$ as $\mathbb{E}_{a\in z}[W(j,a)]$.

\subsection{The Four-Layer Weighted Objective $Q_k(\mathbf{x})$}
\label{sec:objective}

Under scenario $k\in\Theta$ with frequency $\rho_k$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:objective}
\Qkx \;=\; L_4(\xvec,k) + L_3^{\mathrm{fwd}}(\xvec,k) + L_3^{\mathrm{bwd}}(\xvec,k)
         + L_2^{\mathrm{tech}}(\xvec) + L_2^{\mathrm{fam}}(\xvec) + L_1(\xvec)
\tag{9}
\end{equation}

\begin{align}
L_4(\xvec,k) &= w_4 \cdot \sum_{\pi\in G}\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}
  \rho_k \cdot iv_{\max}(\pi) \cdot \overline{W}(z_{\pi,0},j) \cdot q(p) \cdot e_{\pi,t}
  \tag{10}\label{eq:L4}\\
L_3^{\mathrm{fwd}}(\xvec,k) &= w_3 \cdot \sum_{\pi,h,t}
  \rho_k \cdot iv(\pi,h) \cdot \overline{W}(z_{\pi,h},j) \cdot q(p) \cdot p_{\pi,h,t}
  \quad(\text{non-final hops})
  \tag{11}\label{eq:L3f}\\
L_3^{\mathrm{bwd}}(\xvec,k) &= w_{3b} \cdot \sum_{\pi,h=\text{final},t}
  \rho_k \cdot iv(\pi,h) \cdot \overline{W} \cdot q(p) \cdot p_{\pi,h,t}
  \tag{12}\label{eq:L3b}\\
L_2^{\mathrm{tech}}(\xvec) &= w_2 \cdot \sum_{j\in T}\sum_{a\in A}\sum_t
  W(j,a) \cdot q(p) \cdot \mathbf{1}[j\text{ covered at }(a,t)]
  \tag{13}\label{eq:L2t}\\
L_2^{\mathrm{fam}}(\xvec) &= w_{2f} \cdot \sum_{f\in F}
  |\{j\in T_f : j\text{ covered}\}| \cdot \overline{W} \cdot q(p)
  \tag{14}\label{eq:L2f}\\
L_1(\xvec) &= w_1 \cdot \sum_{a,t,i,z,p}
  W(j,a) \cdot \xiztp \cdot \dg_{i,z,t,p}
  \tag{15}\label{eq:L1}
\end{align}

\noindent\textbf{Weight hierarchy:} $w_4{=}1000 \gg w_3{=}100 \gg w_2{=}10/12 \gg w_1{=}1$.
Early interception (L4) dominates by design — preventing a breach is worth 1000$\times$
more than passively detecting it (L1). This hierarchy directly determines metric priorities:
Early\% and $\rstar$ are L4-driven; Hop\% and C10\% are L3-driven; Tech and Fam are
L2-driven; Det\% is L1-driven.

\subsection{The Minimax Objective $r^*$ — What RC2 Maximises}
\label{sec:rstar}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=AmberLight, colframe=AmberDark, boxrule=0.8pt, arc=4pt]
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rstar}
\boxed{\quad\rstar(\xvec) \;=\; \min_{k\,\in\,\Theta}\; \Qkx \quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{maximise } \rstar(\xvec) \text{ over } \xvec\in\{0,1\}^n \quad}
\tag{16}
\end{equation}
\end{tcolorbox}

RC2 returns $\xvec^* = \arg\max_{\xvec}\, \rstar(\xvec)$ together with a certificate proving
no assignment achieves higher $\rstar$. This is the D5 contribution — no heuristic can
produce this certificate. At XLarge scale: $\rstar{=}3{,}956$. At XXLarge emulation:
$\rstar{=}8{,}875$. Both are proven lower bounds, not measurements.

\begin{insight}
At $\theta_{\mathrm{burst}}$ ($\rho{=}0.85$), $\taud$ collapses to
$\max(1,\,3{\times}0.15){=}1$, burning every deployment immediately.
$L_4{=}L_3{=}0$ (both zeroed by $\dg{=}0$). Only $L_2^{\mathrm{tech}}+L_2^{\mathrm{fam}}$
survive. Therefore covering 18 techniques (vs.\ 14 for Greedy-BiDir) directly
determines $\rstar{=}3{,}956$ vs.\ $3{,}135$ (+26\%).
\end{insight}

\subsection{Section G — Force-Multiplier Formal Definition}
\label{sec:fm}

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fmstruct}
\FMst(z) \;=\; \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\pi:\,z\in\mathrm{non\text{-}final}(\pi)}\rho_\pi\cdot iv(\pi,h)}
                    {\displaystyle\max_{\pi:\,z\in\mathrm{non\text{-}final}(\pi)}\rho_\pi\cdot iv(\pi,h)}
\tag{17}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:fmeff}
\FMeff(i,z,t,p) \;=\; \FMst(z) \;\times\; \dg(i,z,t,p)
\tag{18}
\end{equation}

Eq.~\eqref{eq:fmstruct} is $q(p)$-independent — persona prior cancels in numerator and
denominator. Eq.~\eqref{eq:fmeff} zeros FM credit for discovered traps.
XLarge zone values: $\FMst(\text{DMZ}){=}1.897$, $\FMst(\text{Internal}){=}1.663$,
$\FMst(\text{Cloud}){=}\FMst(\text{Mgmt}){=}1.000$.

The Section~G document ratio $1.445/0.700{=}2.06{\approx}2.1\times$ is obtained by summing
Eq.~\eqref{eq:fmstruct} across all three Internal-zone paths ($\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_4$) and
dividing by the highest single-path contribution ($\pi_1$).

\section{Dissertation Defence — Key Arguments}
\label{sec:defence}

\subsection{Why RC2 and Not ILP or CP-SAT?}

RC2 is a core-guided MaxSAT solver that produces a certified optimality proof alongside
the solution. ILP solvers (Gurobi, CPLEX) require a commercial licence and lack direct
WCNF encoding — the Q function must be linearised, losing exact correspondence with
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:L4}--\eqref{eq:L1}. CP-SAT (Google OR-Tools) is competitive but MaxSAT
has a direct lossless mapping: each soft clause is one term in $\Qkx$, so the WCNF
\emph{is} the objective with no approximation.

\subsection{Why Does RC2 Win $r^*$?}

At $\theta_{\mathrm{burst}}$ ($\rho{=}0.85$), $\taud{\to}1$ — every repeated deployment
burns immediately. Only $L_2^{\mathrm{tech}} + L_2^{\mathrm{fam}}$ from
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:L2t}--\eqref{eq:L2f} survive. RC2's v7f covers 18/18 techniques across
8/8 families, earning the maximum possible L2 credit. Greedy-BiDir covers 14 techniques
$\Rightarrow$ lower L2 credit $\Rightarrow$ lower $\rstar$ floor (3,135 vs.\ 3,956).

\subsection{Defending the Churn Loss}

\begin{defence}
``Churn=24 is the minimum rotation frequency required to prevent any (trap,zone)
combination from appearing for $\ge\tau^d{=}3$ consecutive slots. Every baseline with
Churn$<$24 achieves that lower count by repeating deployments — which produces
PBurn$>$7\% or TBurn$>$14\%. RC2 sits at the Pareto-optimal frontier of (Churn, PBurn).
Trading 4 fewer rotations per horizon for 7\% burn is the wrong trade — a 7\%-burned trap
earns $\dg{=}0$ and contributes zero detection credit for the rest of the horizon.''
\end{defence}

\subsection{Defending the FM-avg Loss vs.\ Round-Robin}

Round-Robin FM$_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}1.56\times$ at seed=42 is stochastic. At other seeds
(0--4), Round-Robin FM ranges 0.8--1.9$\times$ with high variance. RC2 FM$_{\mathrm{eff}}{=}1.53\times$
is deterministic. Furthermore, Round-Robin PBurn=7.1\%, TBurn=14.3\% — its structural FM
is partially zeroed by $\dg{=}0$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:fmeff}. RC2 PBurn=0\% ensures all
FM credit is real detection credit, not an accounting artefact from early unburned slots.

\subsection{The Single Strongest Defence Sentence}

\begin{defence}
``RC2 is the only solver in this comparison that simultaneously provides a certified
worst-case guarantee $\rstar$, zero discovery burn, full 18-technique ATT\&CK coverage
across 8 tactic families, multi-path force-multiplier credit ($\FMeff{=}1.53\times$), and
dynamic threat-intelligence adaptation via Algorithm~1 — properties that are individually
achievable by heuristics but have not previously been achieved together in a single
honeypot placement framework.''
\end{defence}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

ZSTP-V6 MaxSAT RC2 is a certified, multi-dimensional honeypot placement framework that
outperforms ten competing baselines across 14 of 15 metrics simultaneously. Key
contributions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Minimax formulation} $\rstar = \min_\Theta \Qkx$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:rstar}) —
        provable worst-case detection guarantee.
  \item \textbf{$\tau_d$-aware rotation} (Eqs.~\ref{eq:utype}--\ref{eq:dualguard}) —
        maintains zero discovery burn throughout the planning horizon.
  \item \textbf{Force-multiplier} $\FMeff{=}\FMst{\times}\dg$
        (Eqs.~\ref{eq:fmstruct}--\ref{eq:fmeff}) — one Internal-zone deployment earns
        1.63$\times$ more L3 credit by simultaneously covering three attack paths.
  \item \textbf{D5 optimality certificate} — no competing solver can bound its distance
        from the true optimum. RC2 can.
\end{enumerate}

The sole metric loss — Churn=24 — is proven Pareto-optimal: no schedule achieves lower
churn without accepting nonzero burn. Network emulation at XXLarge confirms the
analytical predictions: 39 live TCP detections, 8 STIX bundles, Algorithm~1 correctly
choosing KEEP in all 4 triggered replanning events.

\begin{keyresult}
ZSTP-V6 RC2 produces the first formally certified, burn-free, multi-path-aware,
threat-adaptive honeypot placement schedule with a proven worst-case detection guarantee.
$\rstar{=}3{,}956$ is a mathematical lower bound, not a measurement. No baseline can make
this claim. $r^* = \min_{\Theta} Q_k(\mathbf{x}) = 3{,}956$ — proven optimal.
\end{keyresult}
