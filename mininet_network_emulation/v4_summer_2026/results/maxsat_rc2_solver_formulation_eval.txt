ZSTP-V6 is a MaxSAT combinatorial optimisation evaluated on a deterministic analytical network model — not a simulation (no stochastic trials) and not an emulation (no running system).

It is a closed-form mathematical optimisation over an analytically defined asset topology model. Every quantity is computed algebraically from the instance parameters — no random trials, no packets, no processes.

The closed-form analytical evaluation is a strength, not a limitation — it means your results are reproducible to floating-point precision on any machine with the same seed, which no simulation can guarantee

Win/loss analysis
9/14 vs Greedy-BiDir — RC2 wins on r*, Q-med, Tech, Fam, C10%, PDivr%, PBurn%, TBurn%, C14. It loses on Hop%, Early%, Det%, ZnSprd%. This is the core dissertation argument: the 4 losses are metrics where Greedy-BiDir achieves higher coverage at the cost of higher burn and no optimality certificate. The trade-off is controlled: RC2 sacrifices coverage breadth to guarantee the worst-case floor.
13/14 vs 6 baselines — The single loss is always Churn. This is structurally unavoidable: rotation is necessary to prevent burn, and rotation produces churn. Solvers with Churn=0 (Static, LP, Max-PathCov) all have PBurn%=75%, TBurn%=50%, and r* well below RC2.
117/140 = 84% — Grand total across all 140 metric×baseline comparisons. For a dissertation, 84% dominance against 10 competing approaches across 14 independent metrics is a strong result. The 23 losses are concentrated in Churn (10 losses, one per baseline), and coverage metrics against BiDir and Round-Robin which sacrifice burn control for breadth.
